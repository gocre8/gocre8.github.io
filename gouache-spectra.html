Gouache Spectra

Every colour on the ARFD103 chart, measured with a spectrophotometer from 400 to 700 nm. Tap a swatch to see how much light it reflects at each wavelength, and how its curve sits between the two colours it was mixed from.

Looking for red? This chart uses cyan, magenta and yellow. Magenta (M) is its red.

Why not red, yellow and blue?

The red–yellow–blue wheel is the traditional painter's set. Cyan, magenta and yellow are the primaries printers use, and they mix a wider range of clean colours, because each one absorbs only about a third of the spectrum.

The magenta on this chart is the paint Winsor & Newton sells as “Primary Red” (pigment PR170), a cool red.

A warm, fire-engine red is a different pigment, such as cadmium red (PR108) or pyrrole red (PR255). It isn't used here; on this chart its colour would sit between magenta and yellow, near YMM.

The same goes for blue: our cyan is the paint Winsor & Newton sells as “Primary Blue” (pigment PB15, phthalo blue). A traditional deep blue, such as ultramarine (PB29), sits between cyan and magenta on this chart, near CCM.
